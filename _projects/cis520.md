### Overview

For this project, we asked whether models trained on modern antimicrobial peptides could predict the activity of peptides from extinct organisms—an idea known as **molecular de-extinction**.

The main challenge was **distribution shift**: good predictions on familiar sequences might not carry over to ancient ones. We tested whether adding AlphaFold structural features to a sequence model would help it generalize.

[Notebook](https://github.com/pedrambayat/MIC_prediction) · [Report]({{ '/assets/pdf/cis5200paper.pdf' | relative_url }})

### Approach

We formulated the prediction of log2-transformed Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) as a supervised regression task, using the publicly available Database of Antimicrobial Activity and Structure of Peptides (DBAASP) with 25,306 unique peptide-target interactions across 6,233 sequences and 50 bacterial targets.

We compared three approaches:

1.  **Encoder Development**: We implemented an APEX-based Sequence Encoder combining GRU layers with attention mechanisms to translate primary amino acid sequences into high-dimensional latent representations.
2.  **Multi-modal Transfer Learning**: To improve OOD stability, we integrated five explicit structural features (pLDDT, helix fraction, disordered state, etc.) extracted from AlphaFold via ColabFold. These were concatenated into a multi-modal layer to provide the model with geometric context.
3.  **Baseline Comparison & Interpretability**: We benchmarked against tree-based models (XGBoost, Random Forest) and used feature importance to see how much the structural inputs contributed.

### Results

Performance changed substantially when we moved to extinct peptides:

- **Modern peptides:** the sequence encoder reached **Spearman rho = 0.79** and **R² = 0.62**, with comparable performance from XGBoost and Random Forest.
- **Extinct peptides:** on 69 peptides, including 41 validated AMPs, Spearman rho fell to **0.36**.
- **Structural features:** these received **less than 1% feature weight** in our analysis and added little useful signal in this setup.

Adding a few structural summaries wasn't enough to solve the generalization problem. I'd be interested in testing richer representations, such as graph neural networks, that retain more of the predicted structure.
